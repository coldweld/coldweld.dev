Hull wear and repair

Hull wear is the slow loss of drive performance that comes with use, and repair is the work of renewing the structure that damage has taken.

35 numbers, 5 with an outside source

  • A2 numbers at grade A
  • B0 numbers at grade B
  • C1 numbers at grade C
  • D2 numbers at grade D
  • E30 numbers at grade E

Functionality

3 topics

Condition

  • A hull has health, and combat and travel hazards take it down.
  • SHIPYARD > REPAIR puts it back, for a fee and the days the yard's gang takes. A bigger station has a bigger gang.
  • A job longer than a day offers a day's work instead, because a holed hull still flies but lets more rounds reach what matters.
  • Under way, REPAIR HULL has your hands mend hull and drive with hold steel, far slower than a yard.
  • STANDING ORDERS > REPAIR PARTIES sets which job the hands work first, the drive or the hull, and shows the steel and days.
  • A hulk from a Belt salvage lot is restored by the yard, or by your own hands under RESTORE A HULK.
  • Damage is not the same as wear. See RUNNING COSTS for wear.

Trading a hull in

  • The salvage yard pays 45% of the hull price, less the cost of any repairs it needs, plus half the price of every module still fitted.
  • A restored wreck, and every hull the salvage yard sells, carries a salvage title and fetches 30% instead.
  • So a hull loses value the moment you fly it.
  • Buy the hull the work needs, not the hull you want to own.

Maintenance

  • A hull wears with USE and not with time, so a ship sitting at a dock wears nothing.
  • The yard shows the bill as MAINTENANCE DUE, and you settle it under SERVICE.
  • Every two weeks of travel left unpaid drops the drive a step: 85%, then 70%, then 55%.
  • It never falls below 55%, so a neglected hull always still flies.
  • A worn drive pushes less and wastes fuel, so each leg is slower and dearer.
  • Unpaid wear stays with the hull, so a sale pays the yard first.
  • CHOOSE THE WORK runs only when you can pay its whole bill. SERVICE pays what you hold.

Real-world basis

6 points
  • A ship's drive and moving parts wear as they work, so a ship that stays at dock wears little.
  • A working ship costs about a third of its price each year to keep, by one estimate.
  • The largest part of a hull is structure, and renewing structure takes people, time and steel.
  • A bigger gang finishes sooner, but only so many people fit around one hull at once.
  • Major overhaul needs a dock or a yard, and doing it away from one takes longer and overworks the gang.
  • A wreck is only worth the labor and steel that bring it back to sound.

Game and reality differences

6 points
  • The mirror treats maintenance as a yearly job. The game wears a hull with use and not with time, so an idle hull pays berthing and nothing is charged twice.
  • The yard bill does not follow repair labor. The game's repair notes ('The price did not move, on purpose') say that pricing yard labor at the dock wage was measured and refused, so time, steel and gang are physical and the bill stays the old tariff.
  • Combat records damage as hull points, not as tonnes. getdamagetonnes converts points to tonnes until the damage record lands.
  • A yard offers a day's work when a job runs over a day, because a holed hull still flies (SHIPS & OUTFITTING, CONDITION). This is a pacing choice, with no source.
  • Crew under way renew hull with steel from the hold at the same hours per tonne as the yard, but a short gang is slow. The machine shop adds pace and mends nothing alone.
  • A guns-knocked-offline repair and armor re-plate stay instant at the counter.

Source absences

5 points
  1. Atomic Rockets does not provide values for the following:
    1. Repair labor per tonne of hull. 230 person-hours comes from the Butler guide, unverified.
    2. Time to renew a drive part. The drive part share is an open question.
  2. Atomic Rockets and the Navy damage control manual do not provide values for the following:
    1. Wear that grows with use and costs thrust. A 15% step with a 55% floor is authored.
  3. No source on the shelf provides values for the following:
    1. Size of a yard gang. Yard share, hands per tonne, and crew pace are authored.
    2. Price or value drop for a wreck with a salvage title. 30% of hull price is authored.
