In brief

Running dark means cutting power and stowing the radiators so a ship is harder to spot, and the price is waste heat that has nowhere to go.

What the game does

Thermal Signature

  • There is no hiding in space, because a crewed hull always glows against the cold.
  • The harder your drive burns, the further off pirates spot you, so big fast ships get jumped more.
  • A richer fuel grade burns brighter too, because its faster exhaust carries more power at the same thrust.

Silent Running

  • On any transit day you can stow the radiators and run dark, which drops you off pirate sensors.
  • Silent running cuts the day's odds of a contact to 35% of normal.
  • The catch is heat. Every system aboard draws power, and all of that power ends up as heat inside the hull.
  • With every system running, most hulls fill up in about a day, and past that the heat starts to cook the ship.
  • The engineer sheds systems while you run dark, so the heat builds slower and you stay dark longer.
  • STANDING ORDERS > RUNNING DARK sets how many, and quotes what a dark day without each one costs.
  • The air plant is never shed.
  • One day of normal cruise vents it all, unless the radiators are shot away.

Why: the real physics or procedure

  • A warm object shows against the 3 K background of space, so a crewed ship glows.
  • A crew needs a room near 285 K, so even a ship with its drive off glows in infrared.
  • A sensor sees a ship farther as its surface gets hotter and larger: range grows with the square of temperature.
  • A drive burn is far brighter than a habitat, and it also reveals the ship's mass and velocity.
  • Refrigerating the ship does not help, because a heat pump needs power and makes more waste heat.
  • A ship can hold heat in a sink for a short time, so going dark always has a time limit.
  • So hiding is only partial: a dark ship is harder to find without a wide sensor net, but it is never invisible.

Where the game departs from reality

  • The game lets a dark ship cut the day's contact odds. The mirror says no real stealth exists. The code comment says a dark hull is cold enough to slip past hostiles without a real surveillance net (combat/hazards.py, SILENT_RUNNING_SIGNATURE_FACTOR).
  • A dark hull never reaches zero signature, because the crew still breathes (combat/hazards.py). This matches the mirror's point about a 285 K habitat.
  • The signature is a size baseline plus the fitted acceleration. The code does not read exhaust speed or thrust power, which set the real heat of a burn. The repo gives no reason for this choice (docs/notes/hazards.md, 'Thermal signature', which does not explain it).
  • Stored heat is an abstract loop with a fixed capacity, authored and equal for every hull (combat/plant.py, LOOP_POINTS). A real heat sink lasts minutes, but the game allows several dark days.
  • Venting heat on a normal cruise day is a game rule. The pages read give no vent rate for a ship that deploys its radiators again.
  • Going dark mid-fight is a separate rule from the transit order, with an enemy accuracy penalty and extra heat (combat/hazards.py, 'silent running in combat').

Where the sources are silent

  • The pages read give no contact chance for a dark ship and no odds of being found by a given hunter.
  • The pages read do not set how many days a hull can stay dark, because a real ship has no abstract heat capacity.
  • The pages read give no sensor net or area-wide search rule, so the game's contact roll stands in for that.

Sources

  • Atomic Rockets, spacewardetect, What If I Run Silent And Cold?: Detection range for a silent ship is 13.4 times the square root of its projected area times its temperature squared, in km. A 285 K habitat stands out against 3 K. A 1,510 m^2 hull at 273 K could be seen from about 38.8 million km. "will stand out like a search-light" (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, spacewardetect, Surely Sheer Distance Will Hide Engine Burns: A burning drive is seen far away. The example gives 1.2 billion km for a chemical drive and 25 billion km for an ion drive. (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, spacewardetect, Section 3: Stealth Problems Overview: A ship that hides while coasting becomes visible as soon as it burns, and the burn reveals its mass and velocity. Heat sinks are impractical over long periods: each kW of heat needs 1 kg of ice every 7.8 minutes. (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, spacewardetect, There Ain't No Stealth In Space: The page states that space stealth is nearly impossible, except for limited cases such as hiding behind a planet. (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, heatrad, Radiator vulnerability (text beginning 'Solutions for reducing the vulnerability of radiators'): If all radiators are retracted the ship must use heat sinks. That works only for very short periods. (The page's own boil-off time is wrong by about five times, so it is not used: see the heat note.) (all uses)
