Running dark

Running dark means cutting power and stowing the radiators so a ship is harder to spot, and the price is waste heat that has nowhere to go.

What the game does

2 topics

Thermal Signature

  • There is no hiding in space, because a crewed hull always glows against the cold.
  • The harder your drive burns, the further off pirates spot you, so big fast ships get jumped more.
  • A richer fuel grade burns brighter too, because its faster exhaust carries more power at the same thrust.

Silent Running

  • On any transit day you can stow the radiators and run dark, which drops you off pirate sensors.
  • Silent running cuts the day's odds of a contact to 35% of normal.
  • The catch is heat. Every system aboard draws power, and all of that power ends up as heat inside the hull.
  • With every system running, most hulls fill up in about a day, and past that the heat starts to cook the ship.
  • The engineer sheds systems while you run dark, so the heat builds slower and you stay dark longer.
  • STANDING ORDERS > RUNNING DARK sets how many, and quotes what a dark day without each one costs.
  • The air plant is never shed.
  • One day of normal cruise vents it all, unless the radiators are shot away.

Why: the real physics or procedure

7 points
  • A warm object shows against the 3 K background of space, so a crewed ship glows.
  • A crew needs a room near 285 K, so even a ship with its drive off glows in infrared.
  • A sensor sees a ship farther as its surface gets hotter and larger: range grows with the square of temperature.
  • A drive burn is far brighter than a habitat, and it also reveals the ship's mass and velocity.
  • Refrigerating the ship does not help, because a heat pump needs power and makes more waste heat.
  • A ship can hold heat in a sink for a short time, so going dark always has a time limit.
  • So hiding is only partial: a dark ship is harder to find without a wide sensor net, but it is never invisible.

Where the game departs from reality

6 points
  • The game lets a dark ship cut the day's contact odds. The mirror says no real stealth exists. The code comment says a dark hull is cold enough to slip past hostiles without a real surveillance net.
  • A dark hull never reaches zero signature, because the crew still breathes. This matches the mirror's point about a 285 K habitat.
  • The signature is a size baseline plus the fitted acceleration, scaled by the square root of the fuel grade's exhaust speed. A real burn's heat follows thrust power, and the scale is authored.
  • Stored heat is an abstract loop with a fixed capacity, authored and equal for every hull. A real heat sink lasts minutes, but the game allows a dark day or more.
  • Venting heat on a normal cruise day is a game rule. The pages read give no vent rate for a ship that deploys its radiators again.
  • Going dark mid-fight is a separate rule from the transit order, with an enemy accuracy penalty and extra heat.

Where the sources are silent

3 points
  • The pages read give no contact chance for a dark ship and no odds of being found by a given hunter.
  • The pages read do not set how many days a hull can stay dark, because a real ship has no abstract heat capacity.
  • The pages read give no sensor net or area-wide search rule, so the game's contact roll stands in for that.
