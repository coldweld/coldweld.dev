In brief

A hail is a radio call that asks another ship to identify itself or stop, and a boarding is a party crossing to a ship that has stopped or been disabled so it can search the ship or take it.

What the game does

Ways Out

  • [M] | every way out, and why a blocked one is blocked.
  • HAND IT TO THE SECOND | the Second fights on by your standing orders.
  • YIELD | only when the hostiles offer terms.
  • DEMAND SURRENDER | orders every breaking hostile to stand down.
  • CRACK THEIR CHANNEL | names the ship each hostile is on. The last of 3 tries works.
  • A demand or a crack holds your fire for 20 s.
  • BREAK OFF AND RUN | on the helm, a full burn away.
  • You are clear when every hostile is past 3,000 km.

Boarding

  • A raider boards by matching your speed inside 5 km for 20 s.
  • The helm breaks a boarding match unless that procedure asks you first.
  • THE DECK FIGHT | your armed crew and boarding party meet the boarders.
  • You command it, even when the Second flies the ship.
  • GENERAL QUARTERS has the crew suited before they arrive.
  • Win, and that raider has nobody left to board with.
  • Lose, and the raiders fill their hold from yours, the most valuable goods first.
  • They take the ship only if you fought on after the Second's warning.

Why: the real physics or procedure

  • A warship may approach any ship at sea to check its nationality, but it may stop and board only on a short list of grounds.
  • The grounds are piracy, the slave trade, unauthorized broadcasting, no nationality, and a false flag.
  • The check runs in order: a signal to stop, then a boat with an officer, then the papers, and force only if the ship will not stop.
  • A warning shot counts as a signal and not as a use of force, so disabling fire comes later, aimed at the steering gear or engine room.
  • The boarding officer examines the papers and then recommends release, or detaining the ship and sending it in for adjudication.
  • A captured ship goes to a port under the captor's control as a prize for a court, so the capturing crew does not own it.
  • Only warships and marked government ships may seize a pirate ship, and a seizure without good grounds makes the seizing state pay for the loss.
  • A coastal state may stop a ship in its own zone for customs, tax, immigration and health laws, and hot pursuit starts inside its own waters.

Where the game departs from reality

  • Who may stop you: choosehostilefleet lets any flag's patrol stop a captain in any flag's space, but the sources limit a stop at sea to five grounds or to the coastal state's own zone. The repo marks this DEPARTURE, unexplained (docs/notes/hailing.md, demandinspection, B6).
  • Order of force: the game goes from REFUSE to a roll to a fight, with no warning-shot step shown. The stand-down screen's line about disabled thrusters and cold guns implies the disabling-fire aim (docs/notes/hailing.md, B6).
  • Contraband: the hail path pays the whole contraband value and keeps the cargo, while the combat stand-down impounds it and fines 30%. The sources support impound and adjudication, so the hail path is the departure. The repo marks it NOT FIXED and says no source sets a fine, so the figure is a price (docs/notes/hailing.md, A1).
  • Capture bonus: the 25% paid on a boarded wreck is a game number, because no source gives a captor a share. The label was changed from salvage bonus to capture bonus for this reason (docs/notes/fleetops.md, Capture bonus, B1).
  • Prisoners: the Handbook sends pirates to the nearest port for the capturing state's law, but the game pays the captain for a crew and moves standing with the flag. The repo gives no source for the payment and says only that a crew taken alive costs berths and a trip (comment in combat/prisoners.py).
  • A private captain seizing: UNCLOS 107 gives seizure to warships and marked government ships, but the player boards and takes prisoners in a private hull. The repo gives no reason, so this is unexplained.
  • No cost for a patrol that stops you with no grounds: UNCLOS 106 and 110(3) make the stopper liable, and the game has no such cost (docs/sources/unclos.md, What the game does with it).

Where the sources are silent

  • Payment to a captor: the Handbook names the prize court and gives no share, fee or bounty, and UNCLOS sets no fine, bounty or share.
  • A surrender: SORM never says who may strike colors, because Navy Regulations is the document that would speak and it is not on the shelf.
  • Rules of engagement: the Handbook only points to them, and the text is classified.
  • Bounty hunters and bail: no source on the shelf covers them (docs/notes/hailing.md, B11).
  • A feigned surrender followed by a shot (the TRICK row): no page on the shelf covers it.
  • Space: there is no sea, coastal state or zone in the setting, so the articles give a shape and not a map.
  • The Atomic Rockets mirror gives only the jurisdiction point and says nothing on boarding steps.
  • These sources were read through their shelf pages, and the original PDFs were not reopened for this note.

Sources

  • United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), 1982, Articles 33, 105, 106, 107, 110 and 111: A warship boards a foreign ship on the high seas only on reasonable ground (article 110). Only warships or marked government ships may seize for piracy (107). A stop with no adequate grounds makes the stopper liable (106). Hot pursuit begins inside the coastal state's waters after a signal to stop (111). A coastal state controls customs, tax, immigration and health in a 24 nautical mile zone (33). (all uses)
  • The Commander's Handbook on the Law of Naval Operations (NWP 1-14M), 1995 edition, Sections 3.4, 3.11.5.2, 7.6.1 and 7.10: Visit and search runs: summon by signal, boat with an officer, papers first, then force if the ship flees (7.6.1). Disabling fire follows ignored warning shots (3.11.5.2). A captured ship goes to a port as prize for a court, with a prize master and crew (7.10). "does not constitute a use of force" (all uses)
  • Standard Organization and Regulations of the U.S. Navy (SORM), Section 6.3.21 d(1), e(1), f(1), f(6): The executive officer names an examining officer, a boarding officer and a Prize Master. The examining officer recommends release, or detain and send in for adjudication. The prize crew delivers a seized ship to the proper authorities. (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, pirate, Piracy: Piracy is an offense of universal jurisdiction today, so any state can board and seize a ship engaged in it. This is a secondary summary of the same law. (all uses)
