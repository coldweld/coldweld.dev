Hailing, boarding and prize law

A hail is a radio call that asks another ship to identify itself or stop. A boarding is a party crossing to a ship that has stopped or been disabled so it can search the ship or take it.

What the game does

Ways Out

  • [M] | every way out, and why a blocked one is blocked.
  • HAND IT TO THE SECOND | the Second fights on by your standing orders.
  • YIELD | only when the hostiles offer terms.
  • DEMAND SURRENDER | orders every breaking hostile to stand down.
  • CRACK THEIR CHANNEL | names the ship each hostile is on. The last of 3 tries works.
  • A demand or a crack holds your fire for 20 s.
  • BREAK OFF AND RUN | on the helm, a full burn away.
  • You are clear when every hostile is past 3,000 km.

Boarding

  • A raider boards by matching your speed inside 5 km for 20 s.
  • The helm breaks a boarding match unless that procedure asks you first.
  • THE DECK FIGHT | your armed crew and boarding party meet the boarders.
  • You command it, even when the Second flies the ship.
  • GENERAL QUARTERS has the crew suited before they arrive.
  • Win, and that raider has nobody left to board with.
  • Lose, and the raiders fill their hold from yours, the most valuable goods first.
  • They take the ship only if you fought on after the Second's warning.

Why: the real physics or procedure

8 points
  • A warship may approach any ship at sea to check its nationality, but it may stop and board only on a short list of grounds.
  • The grounds are piracy, the slave trade, unauthorized broadcasting, no nationality, and a false flag.
  • The check runs in order: a signal to stop, then a boat with an officer, then the papers, and force only if the ship will not stop.
  • A warning shot counts as a signal and not as a use of force, so disabling fire comes later, aimed at the steering gear or engine room.
  • The boarding officer examines the papers and then recommends release, or detaining the ship and sending it in for adjudication.
  • A captured ship goes to a port under the captor's control as a prize for a court, so the capturing crew does not own it.
  • Only warships and marked government ships may seize a pirate ship, and a seizure without good grounds makes the seizing state pay for the loss.
  • A coastal state may stop a ship in its own zone for customs, tax, immigration and health laws, and hot pursuit starts inside its own waters.

Where the game departs from reality

7 points
  • Who may stop you: choosehostilefleet lets any flag's patrol stop a captain in any flag's space, but the sources limit a stop at sea to five grounds or to the coastal state's own zone. The game marks this DEPARTURE, unexplained.
  • Order of force: the game goes from REFUSE to a roll to a fight, with no warning-shot step shown. The stand-down screen's line about disabled thrusters and cold guns implies the disabling-fire aim.
  • Contraband: the hail path pays the whole contraband value and keeps the cargo, while the combat stand-down impounds it and fines 30%. The sources support impound and adjudication, so the hail path is the departure. The game marks it NOT FIXED and says no source sets a fine, so the figure is a price.
  • Capture bonus: the 25% paid on a boarded wreck is a game number, because no source gives a captor a share. The label was changed from salvage bonus to capture bonus for this reason.
  • Prisoners: the Handbook sends pirates to the nearest port for the capturing state's law, but the game pays the captain for a crew and moves standing with the flag. The game gives no source for the payment and says only that a crew taken alive costs berths and a trip.
  • A private captain seizing: UNCLOS 107 gives seizure to warships and marked government ships, but the player boards and takes prisoners in a private hull. The game gives no reason, so this is unexplained.
  • No cost for a patrol that stops you with no grounds: UNCLOS 106 and 110(3) make the stopper liable, and the game has no such cost.

Where the sources are silent

8 points
  • Payment to a captor: the Handbook names the prize court and gives no share, fee or bounty, and UNCLOS sets no fine, bounty or share.
  • A surrender: SORM never says who may strike colors, because Navy Regulations is the document that would speak and it is not on the shelf.
  • Rules of engagement: the Handbook only points to them, and the text is classified.
  • Bounty hunters and bail: no source on the shelf covers them.
  • A feigned surrender followed by a shot (the TRICK row): no page on the shelf covers it.
  • Space: there is no sea, coastal state or zone in the setting, so the articles give a shape and not a map.
  • The Atomic Rockets mirror gives only the jurisdiction point and says nothing on boarding steps.
  • These sources were read through their shelf pages, and the original PDFs were not reopened for this page.
