In brief

A hostile fleet is the set of ships the world sends against the captain. Each ship carries guns with a limited store of rounds and a spread that grows as the rails wear.

What the game does

Enemy Doctrine

  • Every faction fights to a DOCTRINE. Each hostile picks its target by it every 20 s.
  • It matters once escorts or allies fly with you.
  • Earth | HUNTS YOUR GUNS, whatever shoots hardest.
  • Mars | PRESSES CRIPPLES, whatever is already bleeding.
  • The Outer Guild | SPREADS FIRE across your formation.
  • The Unclaimed | TAKES THE SOFT TARGET, your lightest hull.
  • Bounty Hunters | COMING FOR YOU, because they were paid for you.
  • The contact report names the doctrine before the shooting starts.

The Guns

  • Every gun in known space is kinetic, which means it fires a solid round.
  • A postwar treaty outlawed guided missiles and warheads.
  • A COILGUN aims true. An AUTOCANNON hits hard up close. A RAILGUN punches through armor.
  • A CANISTER fires a shell that opens into a cloud of pellets.
  • A round hits harder when it closes faster, because its energy grows with the square of its speed.
  • Past 3 km/s a round melts on impact, so plate weight stops it more than hardness does.
  • Steel stops a RAILGUN best, and ceramic an AUTOCANNON.

Why: the real physics or procedure

  • A heavier slug is real mass, so a heavier gun carries fewer rounds.
  • A cartridge gun streams rounds fast at small caliber, and a heavy gun fires slowly.
  • A navy sails as a squadron under one commander, while pirates and hunters run in small bands.
  • A worn rail groups worse, so spread grows over its life.

Where the game departs from reality

  • The game fires a gun as one catalog firing every cycle. Each round of an autocannon keeps its ammunition, but the damage a second is kept equal to the catalog, so a slower gun hits harder per round.
  • A hostile is built from the hull pool of its faction and topped up to a gun count by size class. A real ship has a fixed fit.

Where the sources are silent

  • The mirror gives no rate of fire for a heavy naval gun, and no round counts for a railgun locker. The game notes record the real Bofors and heavy gun rates as recalled, not read.
  • The mirror describes pirates as a diverse group of small corsairs and gives no size for a band, so the band limit is authored.
