Life support, food and water

Life support is the set of supplies and machines that keep a crew alive, which means air to breathe, water to drink and food to eat.

What the game does

3 topics

Food And Water

  • Each person aboard uses 2 kg of food and 4 kg of water a day.
  • The Supply department and Life Support modules cut that, by up to 60% together.
  • SHIPYARD > RESUPPLY fills both for a fee, the same way REFUEL does.
  • Run either dry in transit and each hand loses 6 morale a day without food, or 10 without water.
  • Health suffers after 3 dry days of water, or 21 of food.
  • Low morale feeds the same desertion risk that unpaid wages do.
  • Under the Gentle setting both last twice as long, and going without wounds people but never kills them.

Life Support

  • Neither side aims at LIFE SUPPORT. A round that gets through the plate gets inside instead.
  • There it can wreck cargo, kill a crewmate, hole the reactor plant or vent a compartment.
  • Otherwise it takes 8 off life support.
  • At 40 or less life support fails.
  • Every 20 s it stays down, bad air can kill a hand at 15%, unless GENERAL QUARTERS has the crew suited.
  • While it is down or its air plant is cut, your guns lay wider and every hand loses morale every 20 s.
  • Life support is mended before every fight.

Morale And Readiness

  • Each crew member has morale, and the crew as a whole has readiness. Both change how well they work.
  • Set STANDING ORDERS in the Captain's Quarters to manage them.
  • GENERAL QUARTERS | the crew stands at battle stations in suits, so nobody suffocates if a round holes life support.
  • It costs each hand 3 morale and adds 3 fatigue on every travel day it stands.
  • A burn of 1 g or less clears 5 fatigue a day, so GENERAL QUARTERS alone never exhausts a hand.
  • Add a hard burn and a hand is EXHAUSTED at 60 fatigue. Suits still cannot save a hand a round actually reaches.

Why: the real physics or procedure

6 points
  • A crew uses supplies by the person and by the day, so a store is counted in person-days.
  • A person needs food, water and oxygen every day, and the water is the largest share by mass.
  • A ship that recycles part of its water and air carries less for the same trip.
  • No ship recycles all of it, so a small top-up of water is still needed.
  • Air is the fast killer: too much carbon dioxide or too little oxygen disables a person in minutes.
  • A pressure suit and a sealed compartment give a crew time when the air plant fails.

Where the game departs from reality

6 points
  • The game keeps one number for water, 4 kg a hand a day against 2 kg of food. The source figures differ: about 2.72 to 3.09 kg of drinking water, and 26 kg more for hygiene. The game's travel notes ('water and food') keep that ratio so raw kilograms do not enter the economy.
  • Hygiene water is not built. The game's design notes list it as a mirror finding the game does not yet use.
  • Air is not a store. The game treats it as per-fight equipment because air fails in minutes, so the game tracks an integrity score and not oxygen mass.
  • Bad air is rolled once per 20 second round as a chance to lose a hand, and GENERAL QUARTERS shuts that roll off whole. The real harm depends on the leak and the volume, which the game does not model.
  • Life support is mended before every fight (index line). This is a choice for pacing, and the game gives no source for it.
  • The Gentle setting halves the burn and removes death from thirst and hunger. This is a difficulty option and has no source.

Where the sources are silent

5 points
  • The mirror gives no rule for how many days a person lives without water or food. The rule of threes (3 days, 3 weeks) is coldweld's choice, recorded in the game's design notes under 'Stores and the rule of threes'.
  • The mirror gives no morale cost for a short ration or a dry tank. The 6 and 10 morale a day are authored.
  • No source gives how fast a hole in a ship's air plant harms a crew, so the 40 and 60 integrity thresholds and the 15 percent catch chance are authored.
  • The Navy manual gives no time or price for repair and no figure for a suited crew's air, beyond the hood and apparatus times.
  • The mirror is silent on the effect of suits on morale and fatigue, so the 3 fatigue a day at GENERAL QUARTERS is authored.

Sources

9 documents
  • Atomic Rockets, lifesupport, Introduction (person-day accounting): Consumables are counted in person-days, so a store of 30 person-days feeds 30 people for one day or one person for 30 days. "A useful accounting device for consumables is the "man-day" or "person-day"." (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, lifesupport, Eeking Out: A bare-minimum cruise needs about 0.98 kg of water, 2.3 kg of food and 0.0576 kg of air per person per day. The food holds another 1.72 kg of water, so a person needs about 2.72 kg of water in all. (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, lifesupport, Food (USS Wyoming figures): A navy crew of 150 on a 90 day cruise carries food at about 2.3 kg per person per day. The page says NASA recommends 3.09 liters of water per person per day. (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, lifesupport, alternate figures from the NC State document: Daily need per person: 0.83 kg oxygen, 0.62 kg freeze-dried food (2.48 kg with water added), 3.56 kg of water for drinking and food, and 26.0 kg of water for hygiene. (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, lifesupport, Breathing Mix: NASA gives about 0.835 kg of oxygen used and 0.998 kg of carbon dioxide breathed out per astronaut per day. A person in vacuum is unconscious in about 10 seconds, and lung damage is fatal in about 90. (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, lifesupport, carbon dioxide in the air (percent list): Carbon dioxide at 1 percent causes drowsiness and at 8 percent causes unconsciousness. Above 8 percent it kills. (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, celss, Controlled Ecological Life Support System (opening): A closed system recycles air, water and food. A system under 100 percent is still useful, and the page calls over 75 percent a huge help. (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, lifesupport, Water: Two authors figured each person needs 0.1 to 0.25 liters of water a day to make up for reclamation losses. NASA assumes 3.909 kg of potable water and 26 kg of hygiene water a person a day. (all uses)
  • US Navy training manual, "Damage Control" (chapter 12), What it says (General quarters, 12-8) and Life support and fire (12-14 to 12-24): General quarters is the highest state of readiness and an all-hands duty. The chapter lists an escape hood with 15 minutes of air and breathing apparatus rated for about 30 to 45 minutes. (all uses)
