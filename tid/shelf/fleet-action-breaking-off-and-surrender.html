Fleet action, breaking off and surrender

A fleet action is a fight with more than two hulls, in which each side picks targets by a doctrine, a hull breaks off when the cost of staying grows past what it can win, and a beaten hull may yield to a boarding party.

Functionality

3 topics

Enemy doctrine

  • Every faction fights to a DOCTRINE. Each hostile picks its target by it every 20 s.
  • It matters once escorts or allies fly with you.
  • Earth | HUNTS YOUR GUNS, whatever shoots hardest.
  • Mars | PRESSES CRIPPLES, whatever is already bleeding.
  • The Outer Guild | SPREADS FIRE across your formation.
  • The Unclaimed | TAKES THE SOFT TARGET, your lightest hull.
  • Bounty Hunters | COMING FOR YOU, because they were paid for you.
  • The contact report names the doctrine before the shooting starts.

Wing orders

  • [6] | orders for one ship, or Enter for the whole wing.
  • A ship that forms on you flies beside you, and the row says what share of the fire lands on you.
  • ENGAGE | it screens ahead and meets the hostiles first.
  • HOLD BACK | it stays near its leader, guns cold.
  • FOCUS FIRE | it works the weakest hostile.
  • FORMATION | crossfire, line abreast, loose or trail.
  • Between your orders each escort leader flies by their own temperament.

Ways out

  • [M] | every way out, and why a blocked one is blocked.
  • HAND IT TO THE SECOND | the Second fights on by your standing orders.
  • YIELD | only when the hostiles offer terms.
  • DEMAND SURRENDER | orders every breaking hostile to stand down.
  • CRACK THEIR CHANNEL | names the ship each hostile is on. The last of 3 tries works.
  • A demand or a crack holds your fire for 20 s.
  • BREAK OFF AND RUN | on the helm, a full burn away.
  • You are clear when every hostile is past 3,000 km.

Real-world basis

6 points
  • A fleet action is decided by who shoots whom, so each navy trains its ships to pick targets by one rule.
  • A ship breaks off when staying costs more than the fight can win, and a ship that was paid to kill has less reason to leave.
  • Surrender is an offer to the other side, and the other side must accept it for it to work.
  • Feigning surrender to gain a military advantage is treacherous, and the law of armed conflict counts it as a war crime.
  • A captured hull goes to a court as prize, and the sources give the captor no share of it.
  • A boarding party that takes a hull strips it, so the victor can be caught at the wreck by a response force.

Game and reality differences

2 points
  • The game charges a captain's record for a feint as a perfidy that a lawful flag remembers once per flag. The handbook treats it as an offense for a court and names no price.
  • Enemies once picked a target by a flat random choice. A doctrine per faction replaced it without lengthening a fight.

Source absences

4 points
  1. No source on the shelf provides values for the following:
    1. Chances, thresholds, or hit bonuses. All are authored.
    2. Odds and penalty of a feigned surrender. The Handbook calls a feigned surrender treacherous.
  2. SORM does not cover the following:
    1. Who may strike colors.
    2. Procedure to yield a ship.
