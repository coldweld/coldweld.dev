In brief

The aftermath is what the crew's departments do once the shooting stops: the medical head saves the wounded, the deck hands strip the wreck, the supply hands drain its tanks, and the hands who held the corridors are counted as lost or hurt.

What the game does

Boarding

  • A raider boards by matching your speed inside 5 km for 20 s.
  • The helm breaks a boarding match unless that procedure asks you first.
  • THE DECK FIGHT | your armed crew and boarding party meet the boarders.
  • You command it, even when the Second flies the ship.
  • GENERAL QUARTERS has the crew suited before they arrive.
  • Win, and that raider has nobody left to board with.
  • Lose, and the raiders fill their hold from yours, the most valuable goods first.
  • They take the ship only if you fought on after the Second's warning.

Why: the real physics or procedure

  • The fight itself is fast, and the cost of a fight falls on the people and the wreck afterward.
  • A wound takes a person off duty for days, and how long depends on what caused it.
  • A trained medic saves some wounded who would die without one, and a ship with no medical hand saves none.
  • A hull that is stripped yields cargo and stores, but the time it takes can draw a response force.
  • A crew that holds the corridors against boarders takes the heaviest losses of any duty.

Where the game departs from reality

  • Every wound was 14 days whatever caused it, and the 14 had no source. It now varies by cause, with the median days away from work as the nearest real measure of off duty.
  • A casualty to the complement is a number and never a person; only a named crewmate is a person.

Where the sources are silent

  • The Atomic Rockets mirror is silent on recovery times: its sick bay page has anecdotes only.
  • No source gives a medic's save rate, a salvage rate or a casualty share. Those are the game's own.
