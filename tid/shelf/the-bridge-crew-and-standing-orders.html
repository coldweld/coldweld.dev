In brief

The bridge crew is the officers who fly and fight a ship while time runs, and standing orders are the rules that tell each officer when to act alone and when to ask the captain.

What the game does

The Bridge

  • [1] to [4] | the helm, weapons, sensors and engineering stations, each with its own orders.
  • [7] | the drone station, on a hull with a drone bay.
  • [0] | the observer, who watches each contact.
  • [5] | the standing orders. [6] | the wing.
  • [8] [9] | step through the hostiles and the wing.
  • [V] | the camera. [I] | every instrument.
  • [M] | the ways out of the fight.
  • ENTER | pauses the clock or runs it.
  • [T] | its speed: real time, faster or fastest.
  • [N] | skips to the next call from the crew.

The Second Asks

  • Each station is manned by its department, at that department's skill.
  • When two officers want things that cannot both happen, the clock stops and the Second asks you.
  • Each officer states an order and its cost, and the Second recommends one.
  • [O] | sets each kind of question to act and report, or to ask you first.
  • A hull with a complement under 10 hands has no Second, so the bridge asks.
  • The Second flies by your nerve, from the STANDING ORDERS screen.
  • Once a fight, the Second hands the ship back if the crew breaks off or the ship cannot answer.
  • Then you surrender, try to run, or give it back.

Standing Orders

  • The standing orders on [5] are what the crew does without asking you.
  • FIRE RULE | at will in reach, in the kill zone only, or held.
  • DODGE | weave, sidestep, every called round, or hold course.
  • SPARE SINKS | when the crew swaps a cold sink into a hot gun.
  • TARGET | the contact the crew works now.
  • NEXT TARGET | the weakest, the nearest, the heaviest guns, or the one shooting at us.
  • KEEL GUN | the glossary explains its three orders.
  • PROCEDURES | eight kinds of question, each acted on or asked first.

Why: the real physics or procedure

  • A real ship runs its watches through stations that report to a commander, and the commander decides.
  • A crew that handles routine work alone and stops the clock only for a real choice keeps a fight playable.
  • Skill changes how fast and how exactly a station reports, and temperament changes the advice it gives.
  • A hostile ship flies the same crew routine as the captain, so one rule serves every ship.

Where the game departs from reality

  • Every number here is authored for play. The bridge crew has no outside figure, and the notes say so (docs/notes/newtonian.md, bridge.py).
  • A real ship does not stop its clock for a decision. The game does, so the captain can read before choosing.

Where the sources are silent

  • The mirror and the repo shelf give no figure for how often a crew repeats a question, how long a stand-off lasts before a Second calls it, or how fast a crew talks on a net.
