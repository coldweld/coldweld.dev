Heat and radiators

A radiator is a fin that sheds a ship's waste heat into space. It is the only way a ship in vacuum can get rid of heat for good.

38 numbers, 6 with an outside source

  • A0 numbers at grade A
  • B2 numbers at grade B
  • C1 numbers at grade C
  • D3 numbers at grade D
  • E32 numbers at grade E

Functionality

1 topic

Waste heat

  • Waste heat comes from your drive and your reactor plant, and it fills your coolant loop.
  • A drive at full burn makes 1 heat a second.
  • Intact radiators shed 12 heat every 20 s, and damaged ones less.
  • Pumps carry 8 every 20 s into your water tank until it is warm.
  • Your loop holds 32. Past that the tank boils, so water goes out as steam.
  • With the tank dry the loop climbs to 64, and there the drive must go cold.
  • Each gun has its own heat sink, and a gun with a full sink waits until it cools.
  • The crew can swap in one of 3 spare sinks, in 10 s.

Real-world basis

8 points
  • A ship in vacuum has no air or water around it to carry heat away.
  • So heat leaves a ship only as light, from a hot surface called a radiator.
  • A radiator sheds more heat the hotter it runs, and the gain grows with the fourth power of its temperature.
  • Every engine and generator wastes heat, and a machine that turns heat into power adds more waste of its own.
  • A fin is large and thin, so one hit can cripple it, and that makes radiators the weak point of a warship.
  • A warship can carry a heat sink instead: a cold block, such as ice, that melts as it takes heat in.
  • A sink holds only a few minutes of heat, so it buys time for a fight and does not end the need for fins.
  • Boiling water carries away a great deal of heat for its mass, so a ship can burn its water as a last resort.

Game and reality differences

6 points
  • The game keeps heat in one unit, a point equal to one second of the hull's full drive waste. Real radiators are rated in watts per square meter. The reason is one model for both fights.
  • The game's fins are a flat 0.6 of drive heat for every hull, not a computed area. Damage to the fins cuts it.
  • The coolant loop is the tank, and the tank is the ship's own propellant water. Burning water as propellant leaves tank warmth unchanged, since warmth is stored as a share.
  • Fins act before the pumps, so a quiet plant keeps a cold tank for a fight. A first draft pumped first and the tank was full when needed.
  • A gun's sink sheds into the ship through its own small fins, not into the loop. The mirror describes a sink that is pumped out or boiled off, so the game's shed rate is a simplification.
  • The menu fight's heat on the hull was replaced by a sink per gun on 2026-10-02, at coldweld's choice.

Source absences

7 points
  1. Atomic Rockets does not provide values for the following:
    1. Heat loop size. 32 points is authored.
    2. Heat loop ceiling. 64 points is authored.
    3. Share of drive power a ship keeps as heat. The share is authored, and it is the setting's one impossible number.
    4. Fin share of the drive's heat. 60% is authored. A code comment cites heat-pipe panels at 10 to 34% of hull mass, unverified.
    5. Firings a heat sink takes. The page's coilgun carries coolant for 10 shots. Sink shed rates are authored.
    6. Spare sink swap by a crew. 10 seconds and 3 spare sinks are authored.
  2. Atomic Rockets figure not used:
    1. A megawatt boils a ton of water in under seven minutes (Combat Heat Sinks). At 2,594,400 J per kg, one megawatt takes about 43 minutes.

Sources

6 documents
  • Atomic Rockets, heatrad, Radiator Details (Stefan-Boltzmann law): Radiated power equals area times emissivity times the Stefan-Boltzmann constant times temperature to the fourth power, with space taken as zero kelvin. A hotter radiator needs far less area and mass. (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, heatrad, Combat Radiators (the Achilles Heel passage): Drives, power plants and most weapons make huge waste heat, and radiators are hard or impossible to armor, so they are likely the first thing an enemy shoots. ""Achilles Heel" of combat spacecraft are the heat radiators" (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, heatrad, Sikon, Combat Heat Sinks: A warship can pump heat into a cold block of phase change material, such as ice, which absorbs heat by melting. It stores only a few minutes of heat, so it puts a time limit on a fight. (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, spacewarship, the coilgun in the example design: A coilgun makes waste heat too fast for a radiator to take it directly, so each shot's heat goes first into a heat sink (there, boiling coolant). The page gives 413 MJ of heat a shot and coolant for 10 shots. "It must be transferred to a heat sink first" (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, thermodynamics, the heat radiator Achilles heel passage and the thermocouple answer: Heat cannot be destroyed, only moved, and making power from heat adds waste heat. Radiators are therefore the fragile price of any power plant. (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, spacewarshipart, Heat Radiators and the multi-mode reactor passage: Burst power is cooled by open-cycle cooling that dumps coolant overboard, while decay heat goes to radiators. The page says open-cycle wastes coolant at an alarming rate. (all uses)
