Atmospheric entry and the descent corridor

Atmospheric entry is the descent from orbit to a surface through air, where a ship either burns its engines to land or rides the air with a heat shield to shed its speed.

Real-world basis

4 points
  • Aerobraking lands a ship with a streamlined heat shield, called an aeroshell, and uses air friction to remove the delta-v.
  • The ship must shed its speed before it runs out of heat shield, which is burning up in re-entry, or before it runs out of altitude.
  • An aerobraking landing needs no powerful engine and saves a great deal of reaction mass, and a powered landing spends that mass to widen the safe corridor.
  • A re-entry skip, flown by Zond 6 and Zond 7, turned a hyperbolic orbit into an elliptical one. The perigee was not raised afterward, so the orbit still met the atmosphere.

Game and reality differences

2 points
  • The routine landing is damage-free by canon, because a 2500-era hull shrugs off re-entry. A hard arrival bills maintenance and never current health.
  • The Blueshifter dive is an alien-megastructure set-piece and is not a real entry. Its health floors at 1.

Source absences

3 points
  1. The Atomic Rockets landing page does not provide values for the following:
    1. Chance that an entry goes wrong. Every chance in the table is authored.
    2. Price of a hard landing. The days of maintenance are authored.
    3. Fuel cost of a powered landing against an aerobraked one.
