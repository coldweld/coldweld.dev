In brief

Mining is the work of pulling ore out of a rock in a field, which is a place where a crew can find and work rocks.

What the game does

In Brief

  • A field is a place where rock and ice can be worked, and each field holds a finite, real set of rocks.
  • The three belt zones and Saturn's rings carry them.
  • The inner, middle and outer belt are separate crossings, and each yields different ore: metal inward, carbon and ice outward.

Working A Rock

  • Scan for contacts first, then qualify one with a PROSPECTOR ARRAY to reveal what it is made of and how rich it is.
  • Then decide how hard to work it.
  • Push the drones harder and you pull more ore, but you draw attention and risk an accident.
  • Extraction runs for hours and burns fuel and stores the whole time.
  • A rock you work is worked out for good, and whatever danger the field carries does not pause while you sit still.

Other Miners

  • The local stations mine these fields too, so a busy field is thinner and poorer than a quiet one.
  • Anything that knocks local extraction offline leaves better rock for the next crew to show up.
  • The local stations' rigs are out there in person, holding claims on the better rocks.
  • Hail one for a tip, hire yourself out as its guard, or take its ore and take the reputation hit.
  • A rig that finishes its claim takes that rock with it.

Why: the real physics or procedure

  • The real asteroid belt is nearly empty, because it is a huge volume with few large bodies.
  • Bodies wider than 1 km sit about 900,000 km apart, which is more than twice the distance from Earth to the Moon.
  • Most known asteroids are carbonaceous, so they hold water and carbon compounds, and fewer are stony or metallic.
  • A stony asteroid holds a great deal of metal, and the rare metals are a small share of it.
  • The four largest bodies are Ceres, Vesta, Pallas and Hygiea, and Ceres is about 975 km across.
  • Miners must look at a rock before they know its makeup, because the type cannot be told from far away.

Where the game departs from reality

  • The game packs a field into a sphere about 300 km wide with clouds of boulders. docs/setting-canon.md section 6 says this is about 2e16 times the real belt's density, and names it a fiction chosen so a fight can have cover.
  • A field holds a handful of rocks, each tens of kilotonnes of ore. Real asteroids of that mass are tiny, and the setting canon notes that these rocks are 4 to 17 meters across.
  • Pressure is derived from what the stations at a body produce, so a busy field is poorer. This is a design rule (docs/notes/mining.md, 'Local extraction pressure'), not a measured fact.
  • Real Vesta is dry. docs/setting-canon.md section 3 notes that the game's Vesta is not, because a dry 500 million person body would multiply water demand by 27.
  • The named giants carry the game's own belt zones: Vesta in the Inner Belt, Pallas and Ceres in the Middle, Hygiea in the Outer (combat/newtonian/terrain.py GIANT_BELT). Each sits at its real distance.

Where the sources are silent

  • The mirror has no entry for Hygiea. The game uses 450 x 430 x 424 km and 8.74e19 kg, cited in combat/newtonian/terrain.py to Wikipedia '10 Hygiea' (2021). UNVERIFIED by this note, since that page is not in the mirror.
  • The mirror gives no size or mass for the mid-size bodies (Lutetia, Mathilde, Ida and the rest). The game takes shapes from Wikipedia and sets density at 2,000 kg/m^3, which is a chosen number (combat/newtonian/terrain.py).
  • The mirror does not explain the Kirkwood gaps that bound the three belt zones. The game derives them from Jupiter's period (comment in locations/stellarbodydata.py). UNVERIFIED here.
  • No source covers how a prospector array reads a rock, so ratings, grades, and the hidden-grade rule are authored.
  • No source covers extraction pressure, rig claims or guard fees. All are game design, recorded in docs/notes/mining.md.
