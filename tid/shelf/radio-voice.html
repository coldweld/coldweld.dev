In brief

Radio voice is the fixed way military ships word a call, using short set words called prowords so that every listener reads the same meaning from the same sound.

Why: the real physics or procedure

  • A voice call names the station called first, then the proword THIS IS, then the calling station, then OVER.
  • THIS IS means the transmission comes from the station whose callsign follows.
  • Once both sides know who is talking, the called station may be dropped from the call, and a reply may drop THIS IS too.
  • Every transmission ends with OVER if a reply is expected, or OUT if none is expected.
  • UNKNOWN STATION is the proword for a caller who does not know the callsign of the station being called.
  • WAIT is a pause of up to five seconds, and STANDBY does not mean wait, since it announces that a delayed message is about to take effect.
  • Enemy contact reports use the basic procedure with changes from chapter 9, and brevity codes are lists that shorten stock sentences.
  • The publication prints its prowords and its example calls in capital letters.

Where the game departs from reality

  • Only one TACTICAL line follows the full call grammar: the stand-down hail. The other lines carry no THIS IS, OVER or OUT, because the pool is overheard texture that changes no roll.
  • The doctrine lines open with the mate being called, as in {mate}, TIGHTEN THE SCREEN. That matches the called-station-first order of paragraph 520, so it is not a departure.
  • The line MOVING TO INTERCEPT. STAND BY. uses the two words as common speech, while ACP 125 gives STANDBY a narrow meaning of its own. This is a small departure with no stated reason in the game.
  • Caps for the TACTICAL register: the game keeps the state navies' radio voice in capitals and the Guild's in lowercase, by coldweld's decision of 2026-09-22.
  • Earth and Mars speak TACTICAL, the Outer Guild speaks as a working crew, and the Unclaimed have no procedure. This is a setting choice and the sources do not cover it.
  • Cracking a channel in three tries with the last always working is a game rule, so a captain knows the cost before committing.

Where the sources are silent

  • TALLY, SPLASH, HIGH AND RIGHT and WEAPONS FREE: none appears in ACP 125. They are brevity and fire-control words, and the source sends its reader to ATP-01, so the lines that use them are UNVERIFIED and not wrong.
  • What each word means in a gun fight: ACP 125 is the grammar of a call and not the vocabulary of an engagement.
  • A call to a hull with no radio name: the source has callsigns and net names, and nothing beyond UNKNOWN STATION.
  • Civil distress calls such as MAYDAY: they are not in ACP 125 and not in the game's pool.
  • The Atomic Rockets mirror is silent on radio procedure: a search of rocket/*.html for proword finds no page.
  • ACP 125 was read through its shelf page, and the original PDF was not reopened for this page.
