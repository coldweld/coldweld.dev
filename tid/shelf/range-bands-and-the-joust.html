In brief

A range band is a stretch of distance between two ships, and the joust is the single head-on pass in which both ships fire inside the close band, where a round arrives in seconds and nobody can dodge it.

What the game does

Range And The Kill Zone

  • Every ship has its own position and speed, and the range is real km.
  • A round takes time to fly, and a crew dodges a round it sees coming.
  • KILL ZONE | where your fastest round arrives before the target can turn clear.
  • Inside it every gun fires for effect, and the ZONE column says who is in it.
  • Outside it one round flies at a time, and each one seen to pass corrects the aim.
  • No gun fires a round that needs more than 40 s to reach its target.
  • CLOSE IN | on the helm, the autopilot closes and holds the range you name.

The Dodge

  • A round takes time to fly, so a hull that moves off its line makes it miss.
  • WEAVE | the drive burns off the line, changing side every 8 s, until the heat fills the loop.
  • SIDESTEP | the side jets push the hull clear. It spends tank water instead of heat.
  • CALLED | the helm jinks off each round the sensors call.
  • Up close a round arrives too soon to dodge, so the helm turns a face to the shooter.
  • It picks the face whose plate costs least against that shooter's heaviest gun.
  • A steered drone follows you in, so a dodge does not shake it.

Why: the real physics or procedure

  • In space the distance between two ships decides almost everything, and the side-to-side offset matters little.
  • A round fired at 6 kilometers per second takes 5 seconds to cross 30 kilometers and 8 minutes to cross 3,000.
  • A ship that can move can be anywhere in a disc around its last known place by the time a slow round arrives, so a round fired from far away rarely hits a ship that dodges.
  • The disc grows with the square of the flight time, so the chance to dodge grows with the square of the range.
  • At close range the flight is milliseconds and nobody dodges, so every armed ship hits and both ships die.
  • Anything hitting something at 3 kilometers per second carries kinetic energy equal to its own mass in TNT, and the game calls that one Rick.

Where the game departs from reality

  • The range line is one dimension, with no bearing and no flanking, on purpose: a map in two dimensions would make combat a movement game (docs/notes/rangeline.md).
  • The menu fight has no velocity state, so closing speed is not modeled there. The Newtonian fight models it (docs/notes/rangeline.md, closing speed).
  • A pass is a suicide pact by physics and by Austen's choice of 2026-09-21: a 220 millimeter round at a 500 kilometer per second closing carries about 3.5 kilotons of TNT (docs/notes/joust.md).

Where the sources are silent

  • No source gives the game's range bands, opening gaps or the hit chance by band. The mirror gives the muzzle speed and the energy law, and the bands are the game's own arithmetic on the muzzle speed.
  • The mirror gives no initiative rule. The die, the acceleration step and the navigation step are the game's own.
