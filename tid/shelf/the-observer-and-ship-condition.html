In brief

The observer is a person at a lens who says what another ship looks like, and a ship condition is the plain word for how much of a hull still works.

What the game does

The Bridge

  • [1] to [4] | the helm, weapons, sensors and engineering stations, each with its own orders.
  • [7] | the drone station, on a hull with a drone bay.
  • [0] | the observer, who watches each contact.
  • [5] | the standing orders. [6] | the wing.
  • [8] [9] | step through the hostiles and the wing.
  • [V] | the camera. [I] | every instrument.
  • [M] | the ways out of the fight.
  • ENTER | pauses the clock or runs it.
  • [T] | its speed: real time, faster or fastest.
  • [N] | skips to the next call from the crew.

Why: the real physics or procedure

  • A lens resolves detail by its aperture and the wavelength of the light, so a bigger lens and a shorter wavelength see finer detail.
  • A jink shakes the observer's own lens, so a ship that dodges sees less.
  • An active sweep images finer than a passive look but gives away the observer.
  • A report of damage says what the hull can still do and not how a person feels.

Where the game departs from reality

  • The game uses the wavelength of green light for every passive look. A real sensor on a hot hull would work in the infrared.
  • The size of each sign (a mount, a plume, a vent) is the game's own list and not a measured set.

Where the sources are silent

  • The mirror gives no lens size for a warship and no count of cells a crew needs to call a feature.
  • The mirror gives detection ranges but no imaging limit for an active sweep.
