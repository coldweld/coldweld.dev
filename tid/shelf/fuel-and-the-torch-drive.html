Fuel and the torch drive

A torch drive is a rocket that throws water out of its nozzle at a very high speed, so a ship can push at about one g for days instead of coasting on a slow orbit.

What the game does

3 topics

Range And The Destination Tags

  • A trip burns the whole way while the fuel its BURN PROFILE spends allows, and coasts on the rest when it does not.
  • It always keeps enough fuel to land and to break off one fight.
  • A richer THRUSTER GRADE throws the same water faster, so a trip burns longer and coasts less.
  • NEEDS FUEL | too little fuel aboard, so the coast runs longer than the stores. Refuel.
  • OUT OF RANGE: cargo too heavy | even on a full tank the load makes the coast outlast the stores, so vent cargo.
  • OUT OF RANGE: tank too small | even empty and full, the coast outlasts the stores, so a richer grade or a faster hull is the answer.
  • OUT OF RANGE means no SINGLE crossing reaches it. It never means the place cannot be reached.

Burn Profile

  • A burn profile is how much speed one trip buys, chosen before you leave.
  • Every profile flies at the drive's rated thrust, so the fuel spent is what changes.
  • ECONOMY | the cheapest trip: the fuel bill plus each day's wages, upkeep and stores.
  • FASTEST | all the fuel above the reserve.
  • ARRIVE BY | the cheapest trip in all, fuel and days, that makes the day you name.
  • Fuel you do not spend stays in the tank for the next leg.
  • A slower trip eats more food and water and rolls for more contacts, and it can outlast the stores.
  • A hull rated above 1 g strains its crew, shown as CREW STRAIN in fatigue per day.

Fuel And Stores

  • Fuel is water that the drive throws out the back, bought by the tonne at the station's water price.
  • Food and water are scaled by the days and by how many people are aboard.
  • CHOOSE THE WORK lists every service with its bill and its wait, and you pick how much of each.
  • REFUEL, REPAIR, RESUPPLY and SERVICE do them one at a time.

Why: the real physics or procedure

8 points
  • A rocket gains speed by throwing mass backward, so a faster exhaust gains more speed from the same mass.
  • A torch drive has both high thrust and high exhaust speed, so it can push the whole way, flip at the midpoint and brake.
  • A salt-water rocket dissolves a fissile salt in water, so the water is the propellant and the salt is the fuel.
  • Zubrin's 20% enriched design gives an exhaust of 66 km/s, and a speculative 90% enriched design gives 4,725 km/s.
  • Each U-235 fission releases a fixed 202.5 MeV, so a faster exhaust needs more fissions in every tonne.
  • The energy in a tonne of exhaust grows with the square of its speed, which is why the salt charge scales that way.
  • A ship that does not need to hurry can burn, drift for most of the distance and brake at the end.
  • Thrust stays fixed while the tank empties, so acceleration rises as the ship gets lighter.

Where the game departs from reality

8 points
  • The ladder runs far past a published design. The real salt-water figures are 66 km/s and, in the speculative case, 4,725 km/s. coldweld chose to keep one-g trips, so the ladder is a labeled fiction.
  • The top rung is the Epstein figure, a fiction on the mirror's own page. Robinson calls an exhaust that fast wasteful for most solar system routes; the game keeps it so a torch can fly one-g trips.
  • The base rung is derived so a full tank flies the close Mars run at one g. The spacing of the middle rungs is authored.
  • A thruster grade changes only the exhaust speed and adds no thrust. The old design added acceleration and was removed.
  • A grade above the base burns only while the hull's custody rights are taken. Safeguards holds every gram of fissile material, and nobody can grant it.
  • The salt charge is a custody bill, scaled by exhaust speed squared. A physical price was unusable because the upper rungs need more energy than fission can give.
  • Gravity and orbits are ignored in the trip time; a trip is a brachistochrone, or burn, coast and brake when the water is short.
  • The tank holds water equal to an authored 100 percent of the dry mass, so with an empty hold the mass ratio is 1 plus that share. The mirror's torch designs run from 2 to 10, so the game takes the low end.

Where the sources are silent

5 points
  • No source prices salt, custody or a refill, so the Cr figures are the game's own.
  • No source sets how much water a tank holds against the dry mass, so the tank share is the game's own.
  • No source covers a reserve to land and to break off a fight.
  • The mirror has no rule for who may hold enrichment grades, because that is fiction.
  • The mirror does not rank a burn profile, a cost-to-arrive plan or an arrive-by day.

Inspiration

3 works
  • Nuclear salt-water rocket (Robert Zubrin, 1991) (real system): the fuel itself: fissile salt dissolved in water, with the water as the propellant
  • The Expanse (Epstein drive) (book): the top exhaust speed of the thruster ladder, taken from the mirror listing of the fictional drive
  • Sergei Korolev (person): the title Chief Designer, given to the unnamed designer of the torch drive in the setting canon

Sources

7 documents
  • Atomic Rockets, enginelist2, Nuclear Salt Water (NSWR): A 2% solution of uranium tetrabromide in water. At 20% enrichment the exhaust speed is 66,000 m/s (6,728 s). The 90% enriched case lists 4,725,000 m/s. The page says the water is the remass. "considered far-fetched by many scientists" (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, torchships, Introduction: A torch drive has high thrust and high specific impulse, so it can fly a brachistochrone. Heinlein coined the word in 1953 for his story Sky Lift. (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, torchships, Torchship Performance (Rocketpunk Manifesto, On Torchships): A 1,000 ton example with a mass ratio of 2 and a 300 km/s exhaust gets about 200 km/s of speed change. It shows a tank equal to the dry mass is a plausible design. (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, torchships, Constant-acceleration Delta-V: At a fixed thrust the acceleration rises as the mass falls. The page says to cut thrust to hold the acceleration steady. (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, torchships, Low Gear / High Gear (Robinson and Campbell): A 1,000 ton ship with a 10,000 km/s exhaust crosses 1 AU in 10.5 days, flipping at the midpoint, and burns 66 tons. A variant accelerates for a tenth of the distance, drifts for eight tenths and brakes for a tenth. Robinson says a 10,000 km/s exhaust is wasteful for most routes in the solar system. (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, enginelist3, Epstein Drive: A fictional fusion drive from The Expanse, listed at 11,000,000 m/s exhaust speed and 1,100,000 s of specific impulse. The 11,000 km/s figure is the page's conjecture from a mass ratio of 4. (all uses)
  • Atomic Rockets, atomicfuel, The Details: One U-235 fission gives 202.5 MeV, which is 3.244e-11 joules per atom. (all uses)
