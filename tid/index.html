The Infinite Diaspora

by coldweld

The Infinite Diaspora is a text-based, hard science fiction, cassette futurism game set in our very own solar system in the year 2500. You take command of your very own ship and its crew to trade, complete missions, form relationships, and more in a setting that does not care that you exist.

Based in realism: Where the game can follow real physics or procedure, it does so without sacrificing player fun. The physics (flight models, ballistics, travel time), the economy (stations generate real goods, which are then consumed), and relationships (named NPCs have a personality, opinion, relationships with one another, etc.) all follow this principle.

Artificial Intelligence content disclosure

I use an LLM to generate and modify Python-language code. I design the game systems, mechanics, and its overall visual theme. An LLM drafted the first version of the game's text and I am currently rewriting it by hand in order to form a more cohesive story and setting. Music, sounds, and in-game computer model are sourced from human creators, who are credited in the game. The console picture is built by LLM-written code around a human-made model and will be replaced by a human-made picture.

Four Design Aspects

Feature-complete:
Game features must be thorough. Either the whole feature is implemented, or none of it is.
Mechanic parity:
I aim to ensure that whatever the player does, an NPC will call the same code to do the same action.
Realism:
Most in-game numbers are derived from the Atomic Rockets website and real sources, where one exists, in order to ensure an accurate simulation.
Fun:
The most important design aspect of The Infinite Diaspora; fun is a player decision with a cost on both sides, and the player sees the success odds of their decision before they choose.
